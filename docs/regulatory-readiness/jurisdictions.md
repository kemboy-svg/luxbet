## Mauritius

- Regulator: Gambling Regulatory Authority under the Gambling Regulatory
  Authority Act 2007.
- Reported change: the Finance Act 2026 (assented August 2026) amended the
  Act to create interactive gambling licences: B2C (operators such as online
  casinos), B2B (suppliers) and ancillary service providers. Reports say the
  applicant must be a company incorporated in Mauritius and that the GRA has
  still to issue directives for interactive operations.
- Earlier position: the Act provided for interactive licences but that part
  had not been brought into force; offshore gambling by people in Mauritius
  has been prohibited.
- 2026/27 budget measures also reported: removal of the hotel casino
  licence, a wider "digital games" regime, and mandatory connection of
  operator servers to state monitoring systems.

Questions for counsel:

1. Is the B2C interactive licence open for applications, and what are the
   GRA directives (technical standards, server location, monitoring
   connection, game approval)?
2. Corporate, capital, fit-and-proper and local-presence requirements.
3. Whether a demo-only site with no stakes of value needs any licence or
   notification, and what marketing restrictions apply to it.
4. Data protection: Data Protection Act 2017 registration and
   cross-border transfer (hosting is in a DigitalOcean region outside
   Mauritius).

## Kenya

- Regulator: Gambling Regulatory Authority of Kenya, established by the
  Gambling Control Act 2025 (in force since August 2025) as successor to the
  Betting Control and Licensing Board.
- The Act creates separate online licences for bookmakers, lotteries and
  casinos, and requires an approved gambling control system covering
  security, payments, under-age protection, advertising, AML and data
  protection.
- Licensing regulations were gazetted on 30 June 2026 (Legal Notice 111),
  with high licence fees and security bonds reported for online operators,
  a local customer-care requirement and advertising restrictions.
- Reported: a court petition concerning the regulator's leadership; counsel
  should confirm its effect on licensing timelines.

Questions for counsel:

1. Exact licence category, fees, bond and capital for an online casino.
2. Requirements for the gambling control system, server location and
   regulator access.
3. Payment rules (local accounts, mobile money, player-fund segregation)
   and excise duty and other taxes.
4. Data Protection Act 2019 registration with the ODPC.
5. Advertising and marketing rules, including for a free demo.

## Market blocking

If any market is licensed in future, the platform must restrict access by
jurisdiction (geolocation and KYC address checks) for every other market.
The launch gate already requires a list of approved ISO country codes
before it could ever open.
